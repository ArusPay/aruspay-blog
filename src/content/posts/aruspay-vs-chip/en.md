ArusPay vs CHIP comes down to scope. CHIP is a digital finance platform for Malaysian businesses of all kinds: it collects payments, sends payouts, tracks expenses and offers sales financing. ArusPay is built for one sector, Malaysian education, and connects fee billing, the student payment portal and the payment itself in one flow.

If your institution only needs a way to take payments online, a general platform can do the job. If your bursary also issues fee invoices and wants students to pay and get receipts in one place, an education-focused setup is worth a look. This guide compares both using only what each company states about itself.

## What CHIP offers

CHIP describes itself as a platform "built for Malaysian businesses to collect payments, manage expenses, send payouts, and access capital" ([CHIP](https://www.chip-in.asia/)). Its products fall into three groups:

- **Collect**: online and in-person payments, payment links that work without a website, plugins and an API ([CHIP Collect](https://www.chip-in.asia/collect/payments)).
- **Control**: payouts to vendors and partners (CHIP Send) and team expense management (CHIP Expense).
- **Capital**: a Shariah-based sales advance (CHIP Advance).

Payers can use FPX, DuitNow QR, cards, PayLater, e-wallets and stablecoins ([CHIP](https://www.chip-in.asia/)). CHIP lists ready-made plugins for tools such as WooCommerce, Gravity Forms and Fluent Forms, plus SDKs and a REST API ([CHIP Collect](https://www.chip-in.asia/collect/payments)). Its pricing page says it charges no setup, monthly or annual fees ([CHIP pricing](https://www.chip-in.asia/pricing)).

## What ArusPay offers

ArusPay is a payment gateway built for Malaysian education institutions. It is part of a product family designed around the fee cycle:

- **ArusEdu** gives bursary teams one place to manage student fees, invoices and student records.
- **ArusCampus** is the student portal. Students see what they owe, pay online and download their receipt.
- **ArusPay** processes the payment through FPX online banking checkout and moves the money to the institution.

In short: ArusEdu bills, ArusCampus lets the student pay, ArusPay moves the money. ArusPay's pricing is not published, so ask the team for written terms.

## CHIP's published fees

For online payments, CHIP's pricing page lists ([CHIP pricing](https://www.chip-in.asia/pricing)):

- **FPX:** RM1.00 per paid transaction (B2C), RM2.00 (B2B1).
- **Local cards:** 2.0% for credit cards, 1.0% for debit cards.
- **Foreign cards:** 3.0%.
- **DuitNow QR (online):** 1.0%.
- **E-wallets:** 1.4%.

CHIP states that transaction fees are subject to 8% SST and that fees are subject to final approval. FPX refunds carry a fee of RM1.00 (B2C) or RM2.00 (B2B). Because the FPX fee is a fixed amount per paid transaction, it stays the same whether a student pays RM100 or RM5,000.

ArusPay does not publish its pricing or settlement times. Ask for both in writing so you can compare like for like.

## When CHIP may fit

- You mainly collect for events, programmes, registrations or merchandise, and payment links or website forms are enough.
- You want a wide range of payment methods, including cards and e-wallets, from one provider.
- Your website already runs on a platform CHIP has a plugin for.
- You also want payouts or expense tools from the same provider.

## When ArusPay may fit

- Your bursary issues fee invoices every semester or term and wants billing and payment in one flow.
- You want students to see what they owe, pay online and download receipts in one portal.
- You prefer products built around education fees rather than general commerce.
- FPX online banking covers how your students and parents pay.

## Checklist: questions to ask any payment gateway

1. **Payment methods.** Which methods do your students and parents actually use? FPX supports payments from RM1.00 up to RM30,000 per transaction, depending on the payer's bank limit ([PayNet](https://paynet.my/personal-solutions/fpx.html)).
2. **Billing.** Do you need the provider to issue invoices, or only to collect against invoices from another system?
3. **Matching.** How is each payment matched to the right student? Ask for a demo using your own fee structure.
4. **Fees.** Is the fee fixed or a percentage? Who absorbs it, the institution or the payer? Does SST apply?
5. **Settlement and reports.** When does money reach your account, and what reports does finance get for audit?
6. **Receipts.** How do students get receipts, and do you need e-Invoice support?
7. **Integration.** Does it connect to your student information system, website or forms?
8. **Peak periods.** What support do you get during registration week and fee deadlines?

## The bottom line

CHIP and ArusPay solve different problems. CHIP is a broad finance platform for businesses that want many payment methods, plugins and extra tools such as payouts. ArusPay focuses on the education fee cycle, from invoice to payment to receipt. Start from how your institution bills and how your students pay, then compare written terms from both.
